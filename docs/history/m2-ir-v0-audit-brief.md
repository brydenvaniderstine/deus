What the author most wants the auditor to try to break:

1. **A cascade that the 53 sinks do not hold.** Two mistakes that interact (single-mistake proofs do not cover them): a poisoned value reaching a consumer through two producers at once, a poisoned type under an array/slice/struct nested two levels, a poisoned operand under `!`, `&&`, `||` and comparisons chained (D1), a poisoned call argument beside a correct one in a group of overloads or named arguments. Also the opposite: two independent mistakes that produce one error, e.g. a mistake inside a bad assignment target (C11: the target's own mistakes must now appear beside the E0270) and `[1, 2][0] = 3;` shapes.
2. **The ablation proof's soundness** (`tools/nocascade_prove.py`, `tests/nocascade/PATHS.lock`): find a sink the manifest lists as proven whose proving golden does not really exercise it, or a silencing place `tools/poison_paths.py` does not list as a sink (a decision that reads an ERROR type and stays quiet through a path the clang-AST rules miss: a macro, a function pointer, a flag copied into a local). The 18 crash guards are the weakest part: find an input where blinding one gives a wrong diagnostic without a crash.
3. **The fix-it self-check** (`ir_selfcheck.c`) and the E0261 fix: find a fix the checker offers whose result is accepted but changes meaning, or a fix withdrawn that should stand (C9, C10 are known: a withheld fix is not a finding, a wrong fix is).
4. **The muted mechanism** after C11: `group_fits` and `ir_assign_to_pending` are the only dry checks; find a path that records a name use or a call edge while muted and then reports it again, or never.
